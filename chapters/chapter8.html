Chapter VIII. - The key between times

The shadow within the cracked mirror continued to grow until it finally spilled over its edge and began spreading through the temple like spilled ink. It crawled across the stone floor, climbed the pillars, and slowly consumed the crumbling walls. With every passing moment, it grew thicker and stronger. Rowena stood silently, watching the darkness spread across the walls when she noticed something strange. Something was moving within the shadow. She took several cautious steps forward and stared at one of the walls. Unclear scenes flickered within the darkness. They appeared and vanished again before she could properly make them out. Strange faces. Strange places. Some lasted only an instant, while others lingered a little longer before dissolving into blackness. Her gaze moved from one scene to the next. She saw a woman kneeling beside the bed of a dying man. A boy running through a rain-soaked street. An old man sitting alone beside a crackling fireplace. Then she stopped. One of the images was different from the others. On one of the walls, the image of a little girl running through a dense forest slowly took shape. Rowena recognized her. She recognized herself. It was not an ordinary image or an illusion created by the shadow. It was a memory. One of those memories people try to bury deep within themselves but can never truly escape. “You know what to do, don’t you?” Aaliyah broke the silence. “I can see it in your eyes.” Rowena remained silent for a moment. “Maybe?” Aaliyah frowned. “Maybe? Maybe? Tell me you’ve got something better than ‘maybe.’” Rowena continued watching the scene on the wall. “Maybe. The scroll I once read said that the way out might lead through memories. The problem is that the section describing how memories manifest inside a Time fragment was destroyed. I expected visions or illusions, but definitely not this.” Her gaze drifted toward the shadows on the walls. “That’s why I didn’t recognize the shadow.” Aaliyah blinked in surprise. “Wait. Where did you even find a scroll about Time Fragments? I never came across anything like that in the Sanctuary of the Crimson Breath.” For a moment, Rowena tore her eyes away from the image of the little girl. “When I was studying healing magic, I had to help in the library in addition to my studies. Most of it was boring work. Rearranging scrolls, sorting records, and finding books for the masters.” She paused briefly. “One day, while organizing documents, I found a damaged scroll. It had been filed among manuscripts waiting to be repaired or copied.” Aaliyah slowly nodded. “That actually makes sense. You studied at the Tower of Pure Light, and its library is famous across the continent. If a scroll about Time Fragments survived anywhere, it was probably there.” Rowena looked at her memory again. “So each of us has to find our own memory?” James asked thoughtfully. He watched the image of young Rowena for a moment before adding, “Or is it enough to find one and go through it together?” Rowena shook her head. “If I understood the scroll correctly, each person has to find and pass through their own memory.” Aaliyah crossed her arms. “And there’s no other way?” she asked. “Some spell, a portal, anything? Because honestly, I have absolutely no desire to relive the worst moments of my life.” Rowena lowered her gaze for a moment. “The author described what he saw inside the Time Fragment. But nowhere did he mention a single person who avoided their memory and still found a way out.” Aaliyah slowly exhaled. “That sounds like a really bad joke,” she muttered. Rowena gave a bitter smile. “I wish it were.” “So he thought they remained trapped here?” “At least that’s what his notes seemed to suggest,” Rowena replied. Aaliyah nervously rubbed her arm and looked first at Rowena, then at James. “I don’t like this. Not even a little.” “Neither do I,” Rowena answered, taking a deep breath. “But we don’t have a choice. We have to try.” Then she stepped toward the wall and reached out her hand. The moment her fingers touched the shadow, its surface rippled, and a scene from the distant past slowly began to take shape within the darkness. Many figures stood in a graveyard. Because it was an old memory, most of them were blurred and indistinct. At the center stood a man in a dark cloak, tall and upright—Lord Hobs Kastler. His face was stern, but deep sorrow shone in his eyes. He held an urn in his hands, unable to come to terms with his loss. The urn belonged to his son, Henry—a boy who had sacrificed himself. A boy who, on the day of the dark wolves' attack, had not hesitated to face danger in order to save the other children. His courage had cost him his life, but it had also preserved the hope of those who survived. Near Lord Hobs Kastler, barely visible among the shadows, stood a little girl—Rowena, still a child at the time. Her face was pale, almost translucent, her eyes red from crying and filled with grief. She stood apart from the others, as though one part of her wanted to stay while another wanted to run as far away as possible. She was one of the children Henry had saved. “This... is you?” Aaliyah asked. “Yes,” Rowena nodded. “I think this is my key.” Aaliyah frowned. “A key?” “A key to finding a way out of the Time Fragment,” Rowena replied. Her gaze drifted toward the other scenes flickering within the shadows. As though her words had awakened something. The shadows along the walls rippled and began to change. The blurred images grew clearer. Faces that had been barely recognizable only moments ago emerged from the darkness with increasing clarity. “We have to do it now,” Rowena said. Aaliyah swallowed and stared at the flickering scenes around her. “You know what the worst part of all this is?” Rowena remained silent. “I have a pretty good idea of what I’m going to find in there.” Aaliyah smiled without a trace of humor. “And I’m really not going to like it.” James, who had been standing apart and silently observing everything around them, finally stepped forward. Without hesitation, he walked into one of the flickering shadows. Just before the darkness swallowed him, he glanced back over his shoulder. “I’ll see you on the other side.” His figure began to dissolve. Rowena watched the spot where he had stood only moments before. Then she took a deep breath. “Good luck.” And she stepped forward. The darkness rippled around her and swallowed her just as it had swallowed James. Only Aaliyah remained. And her own past. She took a step. And the shadows welcomed her as one of their own. *Passing Through the Key — Rowena's Point of View* Rowena ran. Her feet sank into the soil, and branches scratched at her arms. Behind her ran the other children—four girls and two boys. All of them were equally young. Equally terrified. Equally lost. Then they heard it behind them. It was not barking. It was something far worse — the howl of wolves. Long. Hungry. Moments later, they saw them. They were darker than the shadows themselves, their eyes glowing red. “Keep moving!” shouted the boy leading the group. Henry—the son of Lord Hobs Kastler. Fear was visible in his eyes, but so was determination. The wind tousled his short brown hair, and his once-expensive clothes were torn and covered in mud just like those of the other children. A short sword swung at his hip as he ran, its pommel engraved with his family crest—a gift he had received for his twelfth birthday. “Run!” Henry shouted. Suddenly, he stopped and drew his sword in a single smooth motion. “No!” Rowena screamed. But she already knew she had no chance of stopping him. Henry looked back at her over his shoulder. He smiled. Briefly. Calmly. Then he turned toward the approaching wolves. Rowena did not need to hear a single word to understand what that look was telling her. *You have to live.* More wolves emerged from the undergrowth. Six. Maybe eight. There were too many. The first lunged forward. Henry struck. The wolf howled in pain and vanished into the grass, but the others were already behind it. Rowena kept running. Tears streamed down her face, but she did not allow herself to slow down. She knew that if she looked back, she would stop. And Henry's sacrifice would have been for nothing. The scene changed. Rowena found herself standing in a graveyard, still in her childhood form. And standing before her was Henry. Her breath caught in her throat. “Henry?” For several seconds, she could do nothing but stare at him in disbelief. She was afraid to move. Afraid even to blink. As though he might disappear at any moment. Henry looked at her with the same eyes she remembered. Calm. Kind. “You didn’t leave me behind,” he said softly. “Stop blaming yourself for it.” Rowena burst into tears. “I should have stayed with you.” Henry shook his head. “No, you shouldn’t have. I wanted you to live, and I still do.” “But you died.” His expression did not change. “Yes.” Rowena wiped her eyes. “Then how can you be here?” For a moment, Henry glanced toward the dark shadows surrounding them. “Because a Time Fragment preserves memories. Not only those of people. It can also preserve the memories of places, animals, and sometimes even objects.” Rowena remained silent. “When you touched your memory, you connected with it—and through it, with Henry’s memories as well.” He took a slow breath. “I’m not the real Henry. Just an echo created from the memories he carried before his death.” Tears stung her eyes once again. “So you’re not real.” Henry smiled faintly. “No. But what I’m telling you is.” He fell silent for a moment. “I’m glad I could see you one more time, Rowena. But it’s time to move on.” He stepped forward and embraced her. Rowena closed her eyes and, after a moment, carefully returned the embrace. “This isn’t the end,” he whispered. “Just another step on your journey.” Her body trembled. She didn’t want to let go. She didn’t want to lose him again. But this time, she understood that clinging to the past meant standing still and never moving forward. Slowly, she pulled away. Behind Henry, stone doors opened, leading into the crypt of House Kastler. Rowena looked at him one last time. Henry smiled. The same smile he had given her in the forest. Then she walked toward the crypt, and the moment she passed through the doorway, the world around her dissolved. Rowena appeared in the temple once more. This time, however, it looked different. The shadows upon the walls were weaker than before, and part of the darkness that had permeated the entire temple seemed to have vanished. She had the feeling that with every accepted memory, the Time Fragment lost a portion of its power. But it was not over yet.
