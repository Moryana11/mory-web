Chapter XIII. – Sand and silver

Dawn was breaking when they finally arrived at the Sanctuary Oasis. Aaliyah wiped the dust from her forehead and raised a sweaty hand to shield her eyes. From a distance, the place had looked like a mirage—a dream woven from palm trees, shimmering water, and stone towers. But as they descended the winding path between the cliffs, the dream slowly became reality. The air was still dry, yet the surrounding cliffs protected the oasis from the worst of the desert heat, making it feel noticeably cooler. James walked several paces ahead of them, his gaze fixed forward and one hand resting on his weapon. The slope was steep in places and scattered with loose stones, so Aaliyah kept a steady hold on Rowena's arm to help her maintain her balance. When they reached the entrance, two local guards wrapped in colorful scarves studied them carefully before allowing them to pass. Foreigners were a common sight in the oasis, and the three travelers looked more exhausted than dangerous—though their eyes suggested they carried more than just the dust of the road. "Water..." Rowena whispered when she caught sight of the lake surrounded by fig trees. "And maybe a bed," Aaliyah added wearily, though a faint smile crossed her face. They sat down on the soft sand at the edge of the marketplace. People flowed around them—men in turbans, women dressed in light, flowing fabrics, and barefoot children darting swiftly between the stalls. James nodded toward a cluster of tents. "I'll see if they have any rooms available." As James disappeared into the crowd of merchants, Aaliyah and Rowena watched the vibrant life of the Sanctuary Oasis unfold around them. For Aaliyah, this was not an unfamiliar place—she had visited many times before. She was always drawn to the bustling marketplace nestled among the cliffs, where everything from fragrant resins to strange half-magical creatures could be found. The scent of date palms and exotic spices was deeply familiar to her, and the constant murmur of the market had a way of calming her, even after long days spent crossing the wasteland. Suddenly, a man in a blue turban approached them. He had piercing dark-brown eyes and a firm yet friendly voice. "Aaliyah? It's been a long time since I've seen you here." Rowena looked at him curiously but said nothing. She merely tilted her head slightly to get a better look at both of them, allowing Aaliyah to answer. Aaliyah gave a small nod. "Yes, I've been away much longer than I originally intended." Her voice was warm. "But the Sanctuary Oasis was never far from my thoughts." Aaliyah smiled, though more to herself than to anyone else. "When I was a child, my father used to take me to the Spring of Knowledge," she said softly. "He said that water and memory became one there, and that some people could hear its voice." Aaliyah smiled, though more to herself than to anyone else. "When I was a child, my father used to take me to the Spring of Knowledge," she said softly. "He said that water and memory became one there, and that some people could hear its voice." She fell silent for a moment, her gaze fixed on the lake shimmering silver among the palms. "But neither of us ever heard it," she added. "We only imagined it." Taking a deep breath, she continued. "But fire... fire, yes. Its voice is fierce—sometimes angry, sometimes almost gentle. It never waits. It simply speaks and demands to be heard." Rowena gave her a brief, thoughtful glance but said nothing. Instead, she adjusted her clothing. "Fire never lies," the man in the blue turban remarked solemnly. "It only says what people are too afraid to speak aloud. Just like Aaliyah." Then he smiled and gestured toward Rowena. "My name is Harim," he introduced himself warmly. "It's a pleasure to meet you." Aaliyah smiled faintly before adding, "Harim is one of the people who helps maintain order in the oasis and assists those who come here. You can trust him." Harim settled onto the ground beside the women, and together they waited for James to return. Occasionally, his eyes swept across the surroundings—alert, yet calm. When James finally appeared, Harim smiled at him. "James." "Harim," James replied with a brief nod. Harim quickly looked the three of them over and noticed the exhaustion etched across their faces. "Come with me. I have a place where you can rest," he said, leading them toward one of the larger tents at the edge of the oasis. When they reached the entrance, he pulled aside the canvas flap and motioned for them to enter. "You can stay here tonight. It's safe and well guarded. You need to regain your strength."
