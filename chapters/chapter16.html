Chapter XVI. – Silver in the depths

Night descended upon the Sanctuary Oasis like a heavy veil of silk. The moon cast its pale, mysterious light across the surface of the lake, where it shimmered in uneven ripples. The surrounding tents swayed gently in the night breeze, their canvas walls whispering softly in rhythm with the wind, as though they were breathing. James sat on a rocky ledge not far from the tent, his legs drawn beneath him and his elbows resting on his knees. His gaze was fixed on the distance, where darkness seeped into the sands and stretched its fingers across the silver dunes. The visions from the Spring of Knowledge continued to replay in his mind. A chill crept through his body. It was more than fear. It was the weight of a destiny greater than his own life—a burden as ancient as the desert itself. Behind him came the faint rustle of movement. He didn't need to turn around to know who it was. Aaliyah. She stopped beside him and stood silently for a moment, gazing out across the lake. "James," she said quietly. "Have you seen Rowena?" James looked up at her. "No. I thought she was in the tent." Aaliyah shook her head. "I just woke up, and she's not there." James paused to think. "Maybe she couldn't sleep and went for a walk. After everything we've been through, I wouldn't blame her." Aaliyah nodded slowly. "Maybe you're right," she admitted. "But I still don't like the idea of her wandering off alone." James glanced toward the lake, its surface glittering beneath the moonlight. "We're in the Sanctuary Oasis. This place is safe. Nothing will happen to her." Aaliyah sighed. "I know. I just have a strange feeling." Silence settled between them for a moment. Together, they listened to the wind whispering through the palm trees and the distant lapping of water against the shore. At last, James rose to his feet. "Come on," he said. "Let's walk around and see if we can find her." Rowena sat at the edge of the lake. Her eyes, reflected in the water's surface, saw more than just her own image. She could feel the water moving gently beneath her fingertips, as though it were responding to her presence. It curled softly around her hand, caressing her skin and drawing her closer with a kindness that felt almost maternal. "Rowena?" A voice called from behind her. She turned. A short distance from the shore stood Harim, watching her. "This water..." she said slowly. "It's speaking to me. It told me where I need to go." Harim watched the surface of the lake for a moment. "The Spring of Knowledge is older than we can imagine. It remembers things that time itself has long forgotten. If it truly speaks to you, listen—and let it guide you." Rowena tightened her grip on the orb. Its light was dim now, almost like the steady breath of a sleeping creature. "We leave tomorrow," she said calmly. Harim nodded. "Good. The darkness sleeping beneath our feet is awakening." He paused, his gaze fixed on the dark water. "It is searching for its children." Kayan, a young guard stationed at the northern gate of the oasis, felt a growing unease spreading through his body. Despite the cool night air, sweat slowly trickled down his back. He had the unsettling feeling that something was approaching. Instinctively, he called out into the darkness. "Who's there?" No answer came. Only silence. And then — a whisper. A language he did not understand with his mind, yet somehow knew with absolute certainty. As though he had known it since birth. "Open." His heart began to pound. He took a step backward. "Let me enter." Coldness wrapped around his throat like an invisible hand. He could not breathe. And then the darkness came. The Shadow consumed him. Kayan's body went rigid. His eyes widened — and their color changed. Liquid gold flooded his irises until his gaze no longer seemed human. A deep breath escaped his lips, strange and foreign, as though it belonged to someone else. The shadows surrounding him thickened. They twisted and condensed into a grotesque suit of black armor covered in spikes. A spear formed in one hand, a sword in the other. The weapons were massive and unbalanced. They were not made for mortal hands. Each swing looked capable of cleaving a person in half. And yet Kayan wielded them with terrifying ease. Kayan was no longer Kayan. He had become Zarveth. His expression was empty, his golden eyes devoid of emotion. Cold radiated from beneath the armor, along with a power that did not belong to this world. Zarveth slowly straightened and surveyed the dark landscape of the oasis. Then he waited in silence,for his time to come.
