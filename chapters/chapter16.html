Chapter XVI. – Silver in the depths

Night descended upon the Sanctuary oasis like a heavy veil of silk. The moon cast its pale, mysterious light across the lake, reflecting in the water's uneven ripples. The surrounding tents swayed gently in the night breeze, their canvas walls whispering softly with the wind as though they were breathing. James sat on a rocky ledge not far from the tent, his legs drawn beneath him and his elbows resting on his knees. His gaze was fixed on the distance, where darkness seeped into the sands and stretched its fingers across the silver dunes. The images from the Spring of Knowledge continued to replay in his mind. A chill spread through his body. It was not fear alone, but an awareness of a fate far greater than his own life—a burden as ancient as the desert itself. Behind him came the faint rustle of movement. He didn't need to turn around to know who it was. Aaliyah. She quietly sat beside him without saying a word. In her hands she carried two small, carefully wrapped objects. She unfolded one of them and placed it between them. It was a red stone, smooth and polished like a drop of blood, set within a golden frame—a talisman from her homeland. "For protection," she whispered, her voice barely audible. "A red stone from the Furious Wilds." At first, James simply studied it as it pulsed faintly in the moonlight, almost as if it possessed a life of its own. "Thank you," he finally said. "I think this place—the spring—showed me more than I wanted to see. We're going to need more than protection." Aaliyah nodded, her gaze drifting toward the lake. "Lysara warned us," she whispered. "Morr'kalil—that ancient darkness—is awakening." "And we have to stop it," James added. For a moment, Aaliyah simply looked at him. The silence between them felt heavy. She drew a deep breath and let her eyes wander across the oasis around them. Rowena sat at the edge of the lake. Her eyes, reflected in the water's surface, saw more than just her own image. She could feel the water moving gently beneath her fingertips, as though it were responding to her presence. It curled softly around her hand, caressing her skin and drawing her closer with a kindness that felt almost maternal. "Rowena?" A voice called from behind her. She turned. A short distance from the shore stood Harim, watching her. "This water..." she said slowly. "It's speaking to me. It told me where I need to go." Harim watched the surface of the lake for a moment. "The Spring of Knowledge is older than we can imagine. It remembers things that time itself has long forgotten. If it truly speaks to you, listen—and let it guide you." Rowena tightened her grip on the orb. Its light was dim now, almost like the steady breath of a sleeping creature. "We leave tomorrow," she said calmly. Harim nodded. "Good. The darkness sleeping beneath our feet is awakening." He paused, his gaze fixed on the dark water. "It is searching for its children." Kayan, a young guard stationed at the northern gate of the oasis, felt a growing unease spreading through his body. Despite the cool night air, sweat slowly trickled down his back. He had the unsettling feeling that something was approaching. Instinctively, he called out into the darkness. "Who's there?" No answer came. Only silence. And then — a whisper. A language he did not understand with his mind, yet somehow knew with absolute certainty. As though he had known it since birth. "Open." His heart began to pound. He took a step backward. "Let me enter." Coldness wrapped around his throat like an invisible hand. He could not breathe. And then the darkness came. The Shadow consumed him. Kayan's body went rigid. His eyes widened — and their color changed. Liquid gold flooded his irises until his gaze no longer seemed human. A deep breath escaped his lips, strange and foreign, as though it belonged to someone else. The shadows surrounding him thickened. They twisted and condensed into a grotesque suit of black armor covered in spikes. A spear formed in one hand, a sword in the other. The weapons were massive and unbalanced. They were not made for mortal hands. Each swing looked capable of cleaving a person in half. And yet Kayan wielded them with terrifying ease. Kayan was no longer Kayan. He had become Zarveth. His expression was empty, his golden eyes devoid of emotion. Cold radiated from beneath the armor, along with a power that did not belong to this world. Zarveth slowly straightened and surveyed the dark landscape of the oasis. Then he waited in silence,for his time to come.
