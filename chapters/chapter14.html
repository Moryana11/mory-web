Chapter XIV. – Echoes in the water

The tent was spacious and shaded by thick fabric that softened both the sunlight and the heat. The air inside carried the scent of cinnamon and incense. Soft rugs covered the floor, and blankets and cushions had been prepared for their comfort. With a sigh of relief, Aaliyah removed her cloak and sat down. Rowena stretched and then collapsed onto the nearest cushion. "Finally, some peace and quiet," she murmured, closing her eyes. Her voice was weary but content. After days of heat and sand, she had learned to appreciate even the simplest comforts. James remained standing by the entrance, surveying the area beyond the tent. "Get some rest for a while. I'll see if anyone has heard anything useful." Aaliyah looked at him with a hint of concern but said nothing. She knew there was nothing that could stop him. After he left, silence settled over the tent, broken only by the muted sounds of the marketplace outside. Rowena opened one eye and spoke. "You said you like coming back here. Does that mean you grew up in this oasis?" Aaliyah smiled faintly, running her fingertips along the edge of a rug. "No. The oasis was simply a place we came to whenever we needed peace." Rowena chuckled softly. "So you kept coming back here for the peace and quiet? I don't believe that for a second. You usually go looking for adventure, not peace. Unless there's a handsome man here named Peace." Aaliyah pretended to frown before bursting into laughter. She tossed a cushion at Rowena. "Oh, I'll show you a handsome man!" Rowena easily deflected the cushion and shook her head, laughing. "Am I wrong? Every time we went somewhere together, men practically flocked to you." Aaliyah giggled and covered her face with one hand. "That wasn't my fault! I just seem to attract them somehow. There's nothing wrong with that." Leaning closer, Rowena's eyes sparkled with amusement. "I'm not saying there is." Suddenly, a strange feeling washed over Rowena, and her laughter died instantly. Aaliyah noticed the abrupt change in her expression and leaned toward her. "Is something wrong?" she asked carefully. Rowena shook her head, trying to keep her face calm. "No... it's just that I've been getting a strange feeling now and then." Aaliyah nodded quietly. "Maybe we should lie down and get some rest." Rowena simply nodded, leaned back against a cushion, and slowly closed her eyes. Inside her bag, the orb flickered faintly. As Aaliyah stared up at the tent ceiling, their breathing gradually synchronized with the distant sounds of the marketplace, and before long, both women drifted into sleep. Outside the tent walls, the light of day gradually softened into the amber glow of dusk. The sun slowly sank beyond the sea of sand, and the oasis began to quiet. Meanwhile, James walked through the marketplace, his eyes searching for any signs of unrest. Harim strode beside him. For a while, neither of them spoke. Then Harim finally broke the silence. "It's strange seeing her here again after all this time." James glanced at him briefly. "Who?" "Aaliyah." James did not answer. Harim let out an amused snort. "I remember her when she was just a child. She used to run all over the oasis, climbing places she had no business climbing, while her father chased after her constantly." "I can imagine that." "Back then, I thought she'd eventually calm down." A faint smile touched James's lips. "That will never happen." "No. It won't." Harim chuckled softly. "And honestly? I don't think she'd be herself anymore if it did." They walked on in silence for a few moments. Harim rubbed his chin and let out a sigh. "You know what? I'm not going to beat around the bush." James turned his gaze toward him. Something in Harim's tone immediately put him on guard. "I know how you feel about her. I've known for a long time." "Harim..." Unfazed, Harim continued. "Back then, she let William turn her head. I didn't like it, but it wasn't my place to interfere. She was an adult and had to live through her own mistakes." James remained silent. "Every young person has to touch a hot stove once in a while to learn that it burns." The corner of Harim's mouth twitched upward. "Some people even touch it twice." The corners of James's mouth twitched. "Aaliyah has always been one of the most stubborn ones." "Without a doubt." Harim nodded slowly. "But two years have passed since then. And two years can change a person more than they realize." For a moment, he looked around the marketplace, watching the bustle gradually fade with the approaching evening. "You know, James, I've met a lot of people in my lifetime. And they all had one thing in common." James glanced at him briefly. "What was that?" "They spent far too much time waiting for the right moment." Harim paused. "Do you know what they eventually discovered?" "What?" "That there is no such thing as the right moment." They continued walking in silence for several steps. "People always find a reason to wait. Today they're tired. Tomorrow they're too busy. Next month they have other concerns. And before they know it, years have slipped away." James stared ahead. Harim's voice remained calm and steady. "Fear is a clever opponent. It rarely introduces itself as fear. More often, it disguises itself as caution, reason, or patience." "You think I'm afraid?" "I think you're human." Harim waved a hand dismissively. "And humans are afraid all the time." James considered that. "I'm not telling you what you should do," Harim continued after a moment. "That decision has to be yours. I'm only telling you that when I saw the two of you together today, it didn't look to me like that story was over." James lowered his gaze to the sand beneath his feet. "Maybe." "Maybe." Harim let the word linger in the air. Then he added quietly, "There is a place in the Sanctuary oasis where people go when they're searching for answers to questions they can't answer themselves." James looked up at him. "What place?" A satisfied smile spread across Harim's face. "You'll see. It's not far." Harim led him through a maze of market stalls to a small stone temple hidden beneath a rocky overhang. From the outside, it appeared unremarkable, but the moment they stepped inside, they found themselves in a chamber bathed in blue-green light. "This is the Spring of Knowledge," Harim said quietly. "Long ago, priests came here seeking counsel. Nowadays, only the elderly and those who still believe that water remembers make the journey." At the center of the chamber stood a small stone basin. When James leaned over it, he saw his own reflection in the water...then the image changed. Instead of his own eyes staring back at him, he saw the eyes of someone else — eyes without pupils. James jerked upright, breathing heavily. Harim caught him as he staggered. "What did you see?" "A warning," James replied hoarsely. James walked through the winding paths of the oasis. Dusk had painted the tent walls in shades of gold and crimson, but he barely noticed. His thoughts were elsewhere—he could not shake the image of the pupil-less eyes he had seen in the water of the temple basin. His chest felt tight, and a cold sweat trickled down his back. By the time he returned to the tent, night had fallen. Lanterns hanging from the tops of nearby tents swayed gently in the evening breeze, their lights dancing in the darkness. He stepped inside and hesitated. It was quiet. Aaliyah was just waking up. She sat on the edge of a blanket, looking around as if trying to remember where she was. When she saw him, she straightened. "We need to move on," he said without preamble. "Tomorrow morning. At first light." Aaliyah frowned in confusion. "Why so soon? We still haven't figured out what we're supposed to do with the orb." James met her gaze seriously. "There isn't time for that right now," he said curtly. "I'll explain everything tomorrow, once we've left this place. For now, go back to sleep." Aaliyah's frown deepened. "Are you sure we shouldn't talk about this now? Should I wake Rowena?" "No," James replied. "Let her sleep. We'll all need our strength for the journey, and we both know Rowena has the least of it." Their eyes drifted to Rowena. She had been asleep for some time, yet the exhaustion had not left her face. Her complexion was paler than usual, her lips were cracked, and dark shadows lingered beneath her eyes. Aaliyah drew a deep breath, her expression softening for a moment. "You're right," she said quietly. "She needs the rest." After a brief pause, she asked, "Where are we going?" "To Stonegard castle," he answered without hesitation. "The headquarters of the Stonegard resistance is there. We need to warn the leaders and learn more about the orbs." Silence followed. James turned away from her and stared into the darkness beyond the tent. Deep down, he hoped Rowena would endure the journey to the castle — though he strongly doubted she would.
