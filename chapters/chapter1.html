Chapter I. – The Ruins of Turayne

Turayne had once been a bustling and prosperous city, thriving thanks to the Grassroad — an ancient trade route that wound lazily between mountains and deep forests. Its inns had once been packed every night with travelers, mercenaries, and merchants seeking rest before continuing their journeys. The streets never truly fell silent. Even long after nightfall, laughter, the clinking of coins, and the voices of traders haggling over rare goods brought from distant lands echoed through the marketplace. People believed that as long as the lineage of powerful mages, who had devoted their lives to the city's protection, watched over Turayne, it would never fall. Everything changed on the day of the last High Mage's funeral. On that day, the Shadow Mages of the Sylvean Order attacked. A war erupted—brutal and brief—in which the city's remaining mages fought desperately but futilely against the invaders. Turayne fell, and nothing remained of the city but ruins. Amid the ruins of Turayne stood the shattered Temple of Tevent—one of the last places that still recalled the city's former glory. The stone arches of the ancient temple rose toward the sky like the petrified fingers of gods who had once ruled these lands. The entrance hall, once bright and filled with prayer, was now silent and swallowed by shadow. Time did not pass here—it had sunk its teeth into everything like mold. Every surface was covered by a layer of dust so thick that it completely concealed its original form. The pillars were cracked, some collapsed, others overgrown with black lichens that pulsed with their own dark energy. The air carried the stench of rotting flesh and something indescribable that settled on the tongue like ash. Statues stared out from every alcove—but their eyes had been gouged out, their faces clawed apart. Some were so badly damaged that it was no longer possible to tell whom—or what—they had originally depicted. Bodies lay scattered among the rubble and debris. Some did not move. Others... moved far too slowly. The undead. Silent and twisted, as though they had long since forgotten the purpose of their own limbs. They slowly reached their hands forward, their empty eyes searching for the faintest sign of life. The temple had not spat them out—it kept them. They had all remained here. Priests, pilgrims, healers... their souls trapped within the walls, the floor, and the shadows. And they waited. From somewhere in the distance came the crunch of shifting rubble—soft, yet unfamiliar. A footstep. Then another. Then another. For a brief moment, a silhouette flickered within a dark passage between two collapsed pillars. A sliver of light from a shattered window fell across a figure moving slowly and silently, as though examining every shadow and every crack around them. They seemed to be searching for something—or perhaps trying to make certain they were alone. Her long, straight black hair swayed softly with every step, framing her fair skin like a dark waterfall. Her black eyes, deep and calm as the night sky, held both determination and a quiet sadness. The blue dress she wore bore the marks of travel and dust, yet it still retained traces of its former elegance. Rowena came from Castleton, a bustling port city that lived to the vibrant rhythm of ships, markets, and trade. The scent of sea salt mingled with the aromas of spices, exotic herbs, and fresh produce imported from distant lands. The streets rang with the cries of merchants offering fine fabrics, expensive jewelry, and mysterious artifacts. Castleton was a place where cultures, languages, and destinies from across the world converged. Rowena loved the city with all her heart—it was more than a center of commerce; it was a home that gave her a sense of safety and freedom. Her parents, experienced merchants, managed the family's renowned business dealing in medicinal herbs, many of which came from the surrounding forests. When she was eight years old, an elderly healer visited the city and sensed something extraordinary within her. He recognized that Rowena possessed an exceptional talent for healing magic. After a long discussion, her parents agreed to let their daughter follow her destiny, and the healer took her to the Tower of Pure Light—a prestigious academy where magic was taught in its purest and most powerful forms. At the Purelight tower, she studied healing rituals, ancient scrolls, and the secrets of the Garden of peace, all intended to prepare her for a future spent aiding the suffering. Every day she trained diligently, improving her abilities while carrying both the weight of responsibility and the burden of hope within her heart. She gripped her wand firmly in one hand, its surface adorned with delicate runes. At her waist hung a magical book—an ancient tome filled with rituals capable of changing the fates of countless people. Every step she took through Tevent's Temple felt like sinking into a swamp of memories, a place where past and present merged into a single dark current. Each breath was heavy and painful, as though the very air of the ruins refused to enter her lungs. Yet she continued onward—for the scrolls, for hope. Because even where everything has long since died, there may still lie a key to saving the living. Her mind was sharp, and her heart was filled with the belief that magic was not merely a weapon, but a light capable of illuminating even the darkest corners of the world. And then she felt it. Not the chill of the wind, but a different kind of cold. The kind that crawls down your spine when something without eyes is watching you. Ghosts. Temple priests—formless shadows of the past, without faces or words. Yet driven by an unquenchable desire to drain the last remnants of light from every living soul. Ten of them. Too close. Too fast. Rowena stepped back, one careful step at a time, as the ten ghosts slowly encircled her. Their faceless heads tilted as though studying her every movement, every trace of magic that lingered around her. Their hands stretched forward. Fingers long and thin as needles, reaching to seize her and drain away her life force. Rowena clenched an amulet set with a white crystal in her fingers, cold as ice. It was meant to grant her strength, but even its power could not help her now. Her breath caught in her throat, her heart pounded wildly in her chest, and a desperate thought flashed through her mind. I can't do this... She knew their numbers were overwhelming, their speed deadly. One of the ghosts suddenly lunged forward. Its translucent, decaying body shot through the air with terrifying speed, its claws racing toward her throat. And then something unexpected happened. A blinding light suddenly flooded the temple. A massive pillar of fire descended from above and engulfed the ghost instantly. Its transparent body writhed and twisted within the blazing radiance as though desperately trying to escape. Fragments of dark energy tore away from it and vanished within the flames until its entire form gradually disintegrated. A piercing scream burst from its mouth, but even that was soon swallowed by the thunderous roar of the fire. The remaining nine ghosts attacked at once. But the flames around Rowena formed an impenetrable circle. Sparks rained onto the stone floor, illuminating the temple's darkest corners, while the ghosts hissed loudly and recoiled from the element that consumed their undead forms. The air filled with the scent of burning. Heat spread through the temple, and for the first time in countless years, the ruin seemed alive beneath the dancing flames. As the smoke slowly began to clear, another figure appeared within the glow of the fire, standing atop a stone pedestal. A woman with shoulder-length crimson wavy hair. Her eyes were the same vivid red—piercing and unwavering. Her lightly tanned skin, kissed by the sun, only enhanced an expression that was both alluring and dangerous—a beauty that was not merely decoration, but a weapon. She wore flowing red oriental robes, light and airy, crafted from fine fabric that rippled with every movement like hot winds sweeping across desert dunes. Loose where comfort was needed, fitted where beauty was meant to be seen. Delicate golden patterns were woven into the cloth, catching the firelight with every subtle motion. At a glance, it was obvious that such garments did not come from the cold northern lands. They came from Vienta. The great port city on the edge of the Monolithic wastes, where the sun never truly sets and hot desert winds dance through streets carved from sandstone. Vienta is a city of heat not only because of its location, but because of what flows within its people: fire magic. There, fire is a symbol of life, movement, and transformation. It is mastered from childhood, not as a weapon, but as a living force capable of both destruction and creation. Their magic is as beautiful as it is terrifying. The city's greatest temple—the Sanctuary of the Crimson breath—is dedicated to fire as a force of purification. There, students learn not only spells, but also discipline and philosophy: that fire, like emotion, must be guided by a steady hand, or else it will consume everything in its path. The garments worn by the mages of Vienta are more than mere clothing—they represent the balance between strength and beauty, between chaos and serenity, between a destructive element and the will to master it. And she embodied that balance perfectly. Her stance was firm and confident, her gaze sharp and unwavering. Every movement radiated strength and charisma that was impossible to ignore. She was the kind of woman who did not fear problems—she faced them with fire in her heart and passion in her soul. "Well, that wasn't very elegant, was it?" came a lightly teasing voice. Rowena stood frozen, her breathing still strained. Her body remembered the danger, and her eyes remained fixed on the spot where the ghosts had stood only moments before. And yet, something had changed. That voice. That warmth. That radiant figure. They shattered the suffocating shadow of fear that had settled over her. She felt her shoulders relax ever so slightly, and the frantic pounding of her heart began to slow. Then she saw it. More ghosts lurked within the shadows, but they had retreated deeper into the darkness of the temple. Their twisted silhouettes pulsed faintly, as though waiting for the right moment to strike again. The hateful energy that had seeped from the walls only moments ago seemed suddenly diminished, as though it had lost its breath. The presence of the red-haired woman made them hesitate. The woman smiled, shrugged casually, and then gracefully leaped down from the stone pedestal. She landed with effortless lightness, as though she had not touched solid ground at all, but stepped directly into the rhythm of her own magic. With her staff in hand and heat still dancing around her, she walked toward Rowena without the slightest sign of concern. "W-Who... who are you?" Rowena breathed, her voice quieter than she intended. Thoughts swirled through her mind, but one rose above all the others: I'm not alone. "Aaliyah. Professional trouble magnet. And you're..." the fire-haired woman said with a grin, her staff still smoldering with lingering magic, "...the one who almost died." Rowena swallowed. Her throat had gone dry, but the corners of her mouth lifted ever so slightly. "Rowena," she replied softly. Then she added, "Thank you... If it weren't for you..." "You'd be dead, obviously," Aaliyah agreed with a nod. "But I'm here, so you got lucky and survived. And by the way," she continued as she walked up to her and twirled her staff in a small circle, "this temple has a lot more ghosts hiding around. Want to look around together? Just... temporarily." "Alright. But I'm warning you—I tend to cause more harm than good." Aaliyah grinned and winked. "Perfect. At least I won't be the only disaster in the room for once." Then she leaned closer and added in a whisper, "But if something explodes, I'm blaming you. That's rule number one." They continued exploring the temple long after the last flames had faded. The stone corridors were cold and silent. Only occasionally did the whispers of ghosts drift from the darkness hidden within the ruins. Aaliyah carried her staff lightly before her, its red-orange glow licking across cracks in the walls and revealing shattered, dust-covered altars and broken statues. Eventually, they reached the main hall. Half the ceiling had collapsed, and pale light filtered through the opening above, mingling with the smoldering radiance of Aaliyah's magical staff. The air felt heavy and exhausted—much like the two women themselves. Without a word, they got to work. Rowena methodically searched the alcoves, moving loose stones aside and testing hidden compartments concealed within the bases of statues. If anything had survived, it would have been hidden away, protected from both time and moisture. She opened a secret compartment. Inside was nothing but dust and a few blackened fragments of wood. "Nothing here," she muttered. Meanwhile, Aaliyah tapped her staff against one of the walls. A hollow thud echoed through the chamber. For a brief moment, excitement flashed in her eyes. She quickly pushed aside a stone panel and peered inside. Empty. She exhaled through her nose and slid the panel back into place. "Grand adventure, zero loot. Classic." For a moment, she fell silent, staring at the empty hiding place. "If there was ever anything here," she remarked dryly, "someone faster and smarter than us got to it first." Rowena looked around one last time, slowly and thoroughly, as if checking every remaining possibility. Then she shook her head. "There's nothing left here," she said quietly. "Only empty hiding places." For a moment, silence settled between them. Aaliyah sat down on a broken step and pulled a small piece of dried meat from her satchel. Without a word, she offered it to Rowena. "So, we searched the temple of a dead god," she said with a faint smirk. "And the only things we found were dust and a bad mood." Rowena accepted the food, and the corners of her lips lifted slightly. They sat there together for a while amid the ruins of a temple that had nothing left to offer. And yet, it had not been wasted time. Eventually, Aaliyah rose to her feet, brushed the dust from her robes, and looked toward the exit, where a path wound between the fallen stones and disappeared into the unknown. "So, what now?" she asked, turning her head toward Rowena. "Each of us goes our own way... or do two disasters stick together?" Rowena slowly stood and nodded. "Together," she replied simply. Aaliyah smiled. This time, there was no teasing in it. Only quiet satisfaction. "Alright," she said. "Then let's go find trouble somewhere else." And so their journey began—in the ruins of a shattered temple, among the whispers of the dead and the glow of fading flames.
