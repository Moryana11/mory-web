Chapter IX. – Days from the Ashes

*The Passage of the Key — James's Point of View* Little James, barely ten years old, pressed himself against the cold stone wall in one of the forgotten alleyways of the city of Herba. Rain dripped from his hair and soaked his shirt, which was dirty and far too large for him. The city remained noisy even in the darkness. The cries of street vendors blended with the clatter of horses' hooves and the occasional shout of someone who had lost money, food, or simply their illusions. James held a piece of soggy bread in his hands, something he had found behind one of the market stalls. Sitting down on the ground, he hungrily bit into his first meal in two days. The cold stung his ears, but he tried not to make a sound. He knew that in Herba, the weak were shown no mercy. He watched the passersby, paying attention to every movement and every glance. He learned quickly—who was friendly, who was dangerous, and who might offer a little food to a starving child. Even then, the caution and determination to survive that would later guide him down a path he could never have imagined were already taking root within him. Suddenly, he heard a rustling sound nearby. James froze. A few steps away, a black-haired boy about three years younger than him was crouching in the shadows. Half hidden behind a pile of discarded crates, he stared with dark eyes filled with hunger and fear. Without saying a word, James held out his hand and offered him half of his bread. The boy hesitated for a moment, then nodded and sat down beside him on the ground. That day, they became brothers. James looked after him, teaching him how to survive, where to hide, and how to find food quickly. The smaller boy was slower, clumsy, and easily distracted, yet James remained patient with him and never once got angry. One evening, after the rain had left the streets slick and nearly deserted, they spotted a drunken man lying on the ground in one of the narrower alleyways. The younger boy paused for a moment, his eyes lighting up. He saw an opportunity to take something they could trade for a proper meal. Creeping toward the man, he reached for the coin pouch tucked into his pocket. “Wait!” James whispered, his voice quiet but urgent. “It’s not safe! Stay behind me. I’ll go first!” The younger boy frowned at him for a moment, but the determination in his eyes did not fade. He crouched beside the drunken man and quickly slipped a hand into his coat. As he tried to run back to James and proudly show off his prize, he tripped and fell. The sound echoed through the silent alley. The man stirred and opened his eyes. His furious gaze immediately locked onto the boy, and he grabbed him with a strong hand. The child struggled desperately to break free, but the drunken man was far stronger. James froze. In that instant, he knew he had to act. “No!” the older boy shouted and threw himself at the man. The drunkard refused to release the younger boy. One hand remained tightly clamped around the child's arm while the other shoved James so hard that he crashed onto the wet ground. Then the man began kicking him. Each blow landed hard and painfully. James writhed, trying to protect his face and get back on his feet, but every attempt was met with another kick. Still, he refused to back down. Fear for the younger boy, whom the man still held captive, burned in his eyes alongside a fierce determination to get him to safety. Meanwhile, the younger boy struggled desperately to help James. He twisted in the man's grip, kicking and pushing, but to no avail. When he saw James remain motionless on the ground after another blow, he leaned forward and sank his teeth into the man's hand with all his strength. “Ow! You little bastard!” the drunkard roared. But he did not let go. Instead, his grip tightened even further. The younger boy cried out in pain. Tears welled in his eyes, yet he did not stop fighting. He tried once more to pull free, but it was useless. James lay on the rain-soaked ground, every part of his body aching as he struggled to catch his breath. When he saw the tears in the boy's eyes, he forced himself to lift his head and rasped, “Leave him alone.” The man looked at him. For a moment, he paused and studied him thoughtfully. Something flickered in his eyes, as if an idea had just occurred to him. Then he smirked. “You've got guts, brat.” He kicked James twice more before grabbing him by the throat. Effortlessly lifting him into the air, he dragged both boys to a run-down tavern where the air reeked of damp wood, sweat, and cheap alcohol. With a boy in each hand, he pushed his way toward a table where a hooded man sat in the shadows. Most of his face was hidden beneath the hood, but his eyes glimmered in the faint lamplight. The drunkard leaned against the table and nodded toward the boys. “I heard,” he said, his voice thick with alcohol, “that you're looking for young boys.” The hooded man slowly raised his eyes and gave a brief nod. The drunkard leaned across the table and smirked. “I’ve got two for sale. They’re young, but capable. Feed them a little, and you can do whatever you want with them.” The hooded man lifted his head and calmly asked, “How much do you want for them?” The drunkard thought for a moment before grinning. “Five gold coins.” The hooded man slowly shook his head. “They’re not worth that much,” he said calmly, his voice cold and devoid of emotion. “I’ll offer eight copper coins. Five for the older one and three for the younger.” The drunkard’s face immediately darkened with anger. “What? The older one fought me in the street!” The hooded man merely smirked faintly. His gaze remained fixed on the two boys. After a moment, he tossed eight copper coins onto the table. “Take the money—or keep them,” he said calmly, in a voice that knew no mercy. “Fine. The brats are yours now.” The drunkard quickly snatched up the coins and headed for the bar to buy another bottle without looking back. James and the younger boy were helpless, but James remembered every detail—every blow, every glance, every sound. The hooded man leaned closer, his eyes still hidden beneath the shadow of his hood. “Listen carefully,” he began calmly, his voice deep and uncompromising. “The world you are about to enter is nothing like the streets you have wandered until now. Here, only those who learn to fight survive. Those who are not afraid of pain. Those who know their own worth.” James felt his heart pounding, but the man continued in the same cold tone. “Do not expect mercy. Do not expect anyone to offer you a helping hand. You will be trained. You will be tested. The strong will survive, and the weak will die.” The boys stood by the table, tense and silent. The younger child pressed himself against James while his fear mingled with a growing sense of dread. The hooded man smiled briefly, though there was no warmth in it. “But don’t be afraid. If you endure and learn what must be learned, you will become something worthy of respect. Just remember—survival is a game where pain is the rule and weakness is a luxury you cannot afford.” Then he fell silent, gave a slight nod, and stepped back into the shadows. Yet his presence still hung heavily over the boys. James knew that their lives had changed forever—and that the road ahead would be harsh, merciless, and filled with darkness. The hooded man rose to his feet, took James by the shoulder, and led the boys through the streets of Herba, which seemed even colder and more hostile in the darkness. James held the younger boy’s hand tightly, feeling him tremble, and he knew he had to be strong now—not only for himself, but for him as well. The journey was long and dark. Narrow alleyways gave way to forgotten courtyards and crumbling gates until they finally arrived at a towering stone gate secured with heavy iron bars. The man led them through the city’s main gate, across a stone bridge, and farther along rocky paths winding between trees and cliffs. After an hour of walking, a distant estate finally came into view. When they arrived, they were not admitted through the main entrance but through a small back door hidden among the trees. They did not go up into the manor. Instead, they were led down a narrow stone staircase that descended beneath it. There, a mage and his assistant were already waiting for them. The mage was short, plump, and balding. He wore a friendly expression, but his eyes were sharp and cold—the eyes of a viper that knew no mercy. His assistant, quiet and vigilant, watched the boys sternly through a pair of round spectacles. “Welcome,” the mage said cheerfully, though his voice carried an unspoken threat. “I hope you're prepared.” He slowly approached them and began examining them as though they were not people at all, but objects whose value and potential needed to be carefully assessed. His hands tested the firmness of their muscles, the flexibility of their joints, and the tension in their bodies. “Hm... they're thin,” he muttered as his fingers ran along the older boy’s arm. “They don’t look like they’ll survive what’s coming. We may need to buy new ones if this fails.” The hooded man stood nearby, silent and motionless, observing the mage’s every movement. “The older one,” he finally said calmly, “fought a drunkard in the street. We’ll see how much he can endure.” The mage narrowed his eyes and slowly looked James over before turning his gaze to the younger boy. His expression remained cold and analytical, without the slightest trace of sympathy. “Good,” he murmured quietly. “We shall see.” That very night, they were taken to a laboratory beneath the manor—a cold chamber filled with metal tables, cabinets holding strange instruments, and the faint greenish glow of lamps. The walls smelled of chemicals and damp stone. The first months of experimentation were brutal. The day began before sunrise and ended long after sunset. The mage tested them in countless ways—sometimes through physical trials, other times through psychological ones. Every failure, every weakness, was meticulously recorded on parchment. Dark rituals were performed on them and the other children they encountered there every single day. Before and after the rituals, the boys were forced to drink various potions. Some clouded their minds, others burned through their veins, and still others made their hearts race so violently that they could hear the pounding of their own heartbeat echoing inside their heads. James was often restrained in cold iron shackles, though he barely noticed their chill. The only thing he felt was endless pain. The younger boy suffered just as much. However, his body was smaller, more fragile, and weaker, making every ordeal far harder for him to endure. As the days passed, the boys grew more and more exhausted, their bodies covered in bruises and scars. Then came the day when the mage’s rituals took an even darker turn. The boys watched as more and more children failed the trials. Small lifeless bodies collapsed to the floor, their faces frozen in the suffering they had endured before death. Strange changes appeared on their skin—claws replacing fingernails, bark-like growths spreading across their flesh, as though the ritual had tried to transform their bodies but had failed. James learned to focus on only one thing: breathing. When others succumbed to panic, he repeated the same words to himself: Inhale... exhale... inhale. The rhythm of his heartbeat became his refuge. As long as he could breathe, he was alive. And as long as he was alive, there was still hope. “You see,” the mage muttered one day as he watched the younger boy collapse beneath the weight of another trial, “weakness is not forgiven here.” There was only cold curiosity in his eyes—the detached interest of a scientist observing an insect trapped inside a jar. Months passed. James could feel the younger boy changing as well. His childhood helplessness gradually disappeared, leaving behind only determination and resilience. Pain had become a teacher, and fear a tool for survival. The worst of the trials were over, but peace never came. Only change. Instead of shackles, they were given weapons. The daggers were dull and worn, their chipped edges tearing more than cutting. Every strike hurt their hands, and every fall left behind a new scar. From a distance, the mage observed their training and recorded everything on his parchments—who attacked without hesitation, who defended themselves, and who hesitated. “A hired killer is not a knight,” the hooded man often said while the boys struggled to catch their breath. “Honor means nothing to him. He does not seek glory or recognition. He cares only about the result—a dead target and his own survival.” James soon learned to recognize the rhythm of combat just as he had once learned to focus on his breathing. He watched. He studied. He paid attention to footwork, the angle of a strike, the weaknesses of an opponent. At first, the older boys beat him regularly. But in time, he became the one others avoided. He was neither the biggest nor the strongest. But he was fast. And above all, he was calm. The younger boy no longer trembled when holding a dagger. On the contrary, his fingers gripped it with confidence, as though it were an extension of his own hand. All that remained in his eyes was a raw, untamed fury that flared whenever he smelled blood. He was James’s complete opposite. Where James observed, he acted. There was something wild inside him, something that refused to submit to discipline. While James learned to kill out of necessity, the boy was already beginning to find a twisted satisfaction in it. And the hooded man saw it. Instead of suppressing it, he smiled. Because that was exactly what he wanted them to become: Two daggers. The training was merciless. In the middle of the night, they would be awakened by orders to kill shadows conjured by the mage—illusions of people, animals, and demons. Every movement had to be lethal. Every strike had to be precise. Those who hesitated were beaten or denied food. Those who rebelled disappeared. No one ever asked where they had gone. After some time, the boys became figures dressed in black cloaks and black masks, moving silently like specters. The hooded man taught them that killing was not enough—they had to remain unseen, leave no trace, and know how to wait. “Killing,” he would say as he walked among them with his hands clasped behind his back, “is not an art. The art is making sure no one ever learns it happened.” And in the silent underground halls, where the screams of dying children had once echoed, there was now only the sound of weapons striking wood and the muted breathing of those who had learned how to survive. James was becoming one of the best. Not because he wanted to. But because he no longer knew how to be anything else. Years passed. The boys became young men. They moved with a grace that even seasoned swordsmen might have envied—silent, precise, and cold. One day, the hooded man summoned them. He stood beside a stone table in a chamber so quiet that they could hear droplets of water falling from the ceiling. Resting on the stone were four black daggers. Their blades gleamed as though they absorbed light rather than reflected it. James and his brother stood opposite him. They asked no questions. Both of them knew the time had come. The hooded man slowly ran his fingers along one of the blades, almost as if he were caressing it. “You have trained for a long time,” he said in a muffled voice distorted by his mask. “You have spent years learning how to survive. Now we will see whether you are capable of more than that.” He extended his hand. Two daggers were given to each of them. The cold metal felt heavier in their palms than they had expected. “Today, you will kill for the first time. Not for me,” he continued, “but for yourselves. For the past that made you what you are.” James did not move. He merely tightened his grip on the hilts. His brother, on the other hand, smirked. The familiar predatory smile appeared at the corner of his mouth. “Who?” he asked. The hooded man was silent for a moment. “The man who sold you,” he replied simply. The air seemed to grow heavier. Even without another word, they understood. That day, they would not be killing because they had been ordered to. They would be killing because of the thing that had turned them into monsters. The night they set out was just as cold as the night they had been sold. Rain hammered against the rooftops, and the streets lay deserted. Their target lived in a small village beyond the city walls, in a crumbling cottage surrounded by the stench of cheap liquor. He was a broken man—once brutal, now little more than a shadow of his former self. James’s brother smirked when he spotted him through a cracked window. “So this is what we survived for?” he whispered. “Pathetic.” James said nothing. His hand did not even twitch. He simply watched as his brother crouched low and silently slipped inside. The sound of a footstep. A muffled gasp. And then—silence. James entered a moment later. The air inside smelled of blood and alcohol. The man lay sprawled across the floor while James’s brother stood over him, raising a dagger for another strike. There was more than hatred in his eyes. There was a fury that had slept for years and had finally awakened—raw, uncontrollable, and consuming. It mingled with contempt and a deep, visceral disgust. His handsome face was twisted with rage, his teeth clenched, his breathing ragged. Every breath felt like a scream of suppressed anger. And James... James simply stood there and watched. The hatred burning in his brother’s eyes was reflected in his own—not with the same intensity, but as a shadow of it. Cold. Quiet. Devoid of passion. “That’s enough,” James said softly. His brother turned toward him. “Enough? I don’t know that word.” The dagger came down again. And again. And again. Of the man who had once sold them, nothing remained but a heap of flesh and broken bones. There was no struggle in it. No redemption. Only violence returning to the place from which it had come. James watched. He did not try to stop him. He simply stood there, breathing—in, out, in, out—and thought that perhaps this moment was not an ending. Perhaps it was a beginning. Before they left the cottage, both of them put on their masks. Neither spoke. There was nothing left to say. The reason they had come had been fulfilled. They returned to the Crimson manor through the back entrance. In the underground chambers of the manor, a familiar room awaited them. The hooded man sat at a table. His mask concealed his expression, yet his presence was just as imposing as it had been the first time they met him. “The mission is complete,” James said quietly, though his voice remained firm. His brother stood beside him, breathing heavily, his eyes still burning with the remnants of his fury. The masked man merely leaned forward, his hands resting on the table. For a moment, he observed them in silence. “Good,” he finally said, his voice calm but filled with authority. “That was only the beginning. A second task awaits you.” James’s brother straightened immediately, excitement flashing in his eyes. “A second task?” he asked eagerly, his hand instinctively drifting toward one of his daggers. James remained silent, watching the man behind the table. Unlike his brother, he showed no excitement. He simply waited to hear what would come next. The masked man tilted his head. “Yes. The first task was merely a test. This is the real mission.” James nodded, his expression unchanged, revealing no emotion. His brother, however, tightened his grip around the hilt of his dagger, ready to face whatever awaited them. “Everything you have done so far was only preparation,” the masked man continued. “Now you must prove yourselves capable of more. It is time to discover whether you truly have what it takes.” His brother snorted. “We’re ready. Hopefully this time we won’t have to drown ourselves in the blood of some drunken wretches.” The masked man chuckled softly. The sound was deep and cold, echoing through the stone walls of the underground chamber. “No,” he replied at last, his voice calm but piercing. “This time, it won’t be wretches.” The masked man rose to his feet and gave a slight nod. “Follow me,” he said calmly, leaving no room for refusal. The brothers followed him down a narrow stone staircase that descended deeper into the underground. The stairs eventually ended, opening into a long, damp corridor. Its walls were dark, covered with dust and patches of moss. Smaller passageways branched off from it—narrow, winding tunnels that seemed to lead nowhere, or perhaps everywhere at once. The masked man moved with confidence. He knew every turn and every hidden corner of the underground complex as though it were his own home. The brothers stayed close behind him, their eyes drifting more often toward the shadows lurking in the side passages than toward his back. Each narrow corridor carried the feeling of a trap, and every bend suggested danger that might leap from the darkness at any moment. After several minutes, during which the corridors seemed endless, they reached a larger chamber. From there, a set of broad doors opened into a training hall. Torches illuminated the stone floor and the wooden training dummies arranged throughout the room. The brothers noticed a balcony high above them—and upon it stood a group of masked figures, silently watching them with keen interest. The masked man stopped, and his deep, unyielding voice shattered the silence. “This is your true test. You must face one another. The one who survives will become a member of the Crimson Bloodline. The one who falls... will disappear forever.” The brothers stood frozen. Their breathing quickened as their eyes met—and within those glances was a mixture of shock, disbelief, revulsion, and hidden terror. “What... what do you mean?” the younger brother whispered, his voice hoarse. “Us... against each other?” The masked man merely nodded without moving. “Yes. Your skills, your strength, your determination—everything must now be proven. The one who survives will become one of us. The other... will not.” James stood perfectly still, as though the masked man's words were merely another step on a path he had always known he would have to walk. His brother, however, tightened his grip on his dagger as his heart pounded in his chest. “That... that's impossible,” he breathed. “We can't... he's my brother...” “That is irrelevant here,” the masked man said coldly. “Once you step onto the training ground, each of you stands alone. The rules are simple: survive, and you become part of the Crimson Bloodline. Decide accordingly.” The masked figures on the balcony continued to watch in silence. Their presence was a quiet reminder of the merciless fate now hanging over the brothers. Slowly, James turned toward his younger brother. His face remained calm—unnaturally calm, as though every storm within him had suddenly fallen silent. In his eyes was something his brother had not expected: determination, cold resolve, and quiet compassion all at once. “Kill me,” he said softly. “And live.” His brother stared at him as if he had misheard. “What?” he blurted out. His hand trembled, and the dagger slipped from his fingers. “Listen to me,” James continued, even more calmly. “If you want to live—if you want a chance—you have to do it. This is the price.” His voice held neither urgency nor pleading. It was a final verdict, as though he had already calculated exactly what had to be sacrificed. The younger brother felt something twist inside his chest—anger toward the masked man, a sense of betrayal, and beneath it all, gratitude for everything James had ever done for him. The masked man on the balcony leaned forward slightly, as though enjoying the spectacle. “Choose,” he called down coldly. “Time waits for no one.” The younger brother looked back at James—at his calm expression, at the silent command in his eyes. There was more than a challenge in that gaze. There was reassurance. A promise that everything would somehow be all right. “Why... why are you doing this?” he finally whispered. James did not look away. “To protect you,” he answered simply. “So you can become something more than a victim in their games. Do it and live. If you don't... we'll both die for nothing.” Someone on the balcony laughed—a brief, almost imperceptible sound. Time seemed to shrink into a single moment. The younger brother took a step forward. Slowly. Hesitantly. As though every movement caused him pain. He took a second step, then a third... And silence settled between them, broken only by the dripping of water and the beating of two hearts. James did not move. He stood perfectly still, his daggers still sheathed at his waist, making no attempt to defend himself. His gaze was calm, accepting. Ready to die if it meant his brother would live. The younger brother raised his dagger. His hand trembled. He took another step... Then suddenly broke into a run. The cry that escaped his lips was a mixture of despair and fury directed at the entire world. He charged straight at James— but at the very last moment, he changed direction. Instead of his brother, he hurled himself at the masked man. The dagger flashed in the torchlight. Then struck. Once. Twice. Again. And again. The masked man did not even have time to scream. Caught completely off guard, he could not comprehend that the rules of his own game had just been shattered. Blood splattered across the stone floor, droplets striking the ground like echoes of a decision that could no longer be undone. The younger brother straightened, the dagger still clenched in his hand as his breathing gradually slowed. Then he laughed. A deep, raw laugh filled with rage and release. At first it was short and mocking. Then it grew louder until the entire underground training hall echoed with his scorn. “You think I'm going to play your game?” he shouted toward the masked figures on the balcony, his voice dripping with anger and irony. “You want me to murder my own brother just to survive? Go to hell! I'd rather die myself!” The masked figures on the balcony froze. Some tightened their grip on their weapons. Others simply stood there, stunned and speechless. A silence sharp enough to cut settled over the hall. James remained motionless, his eyes widened in shock, his breath caught in his throat. Then laughter echoed from the balcony. Deep. Genuine. Amused. The man who had been directing the entire ordeal leaned over the railing and laughed openly. “I like you boys,” he called down with obvious pleasure. “You're like a pair of daggers. One is good, but two are better. Both of you will live.” The atmosphere changed instantly. The tension dissolved into a strange, heavy sense of disbelief. The younger brother drew a deep breath. His laughter gradually faded, leaving a mixture of relief and pride on his face. The air in the underground chamber seemed to tremble. The masked man's words continued to echo through the hall while both brothers stood in silence, unable to fully comprehend what they had just heard. “You may leave,” the man added calmly, a tone utterly at odds with the corpse of his dead subordinate lying nearby. “Return to your quarters. Rest. Tomorrow is a new day.” The brothers bowed and left through the corridor. Their footsteps echoed hollowly against the stone floor while water dripped from the ceiling in rhythm with their breathing. Neither spoke. The silence between them was heavy, filled with unspoken fear, guilt, and an incomprehensible calm that followed the storm. When they entered the barracks, the air seemed heavier there than anywhere else. Several other young men sat on their beds, exhausted, wearing identical dark clothing and staring into nothingness. When they saw the brothers, they lifted their heads. At once, the room fell silent. “How... is that possible?” one of them breathed. “We... we had to kill our partners.” James looked at him quietly, without a trace of pride. “We refused,” he said slowly. “They told us to fight each other. We refused to kill one another.” The room became utterly still. “We proved that both of us were useful,” James continued. “And they let us live.” His words landed among them like stones dropped into still water. Confusion, anger, and guilt mixed together in the eyes of the others. Then it happened. An outburst. One of the young men, his hand trembling and his gaze wavering between despair and madness, shouted: “That’s not fair!” He reached for his belt, drew a dagger, and gripped it tightly in his shaking hand. “They kept telling us there was no other way!” he screamed. “That one of us had to die!” Before anyone could stop him, he charged straight at James. “And now you’re telling me you just refused?!” he shouted, his voice broken by pain and hysteria. “That my friend died for nothing?!” James did not have time to react. He only caught a glimpse of his younger brother throwing himself in front of him. A flash of steel. A sudden impact. Then pain. The dagger sank deep. “No!” James cried, catching him before he could hit the ground. Blood spilled between his fingers, warm and horribly real. “No, no, no... not this...” The younger brother trembled. His eyes lifted to meet James’s. Everyone around them froze. No one moved. Time itself seemed to stop. “You mustn’t... blame yourself,” he whispered. His voice was growing weaker, but his gaze remained clear. “You were willing to die to protect me. Now... I’ve simply returned the favor.” James felt his heart pounding in his chest. Then something changed. The world around him began to blur. The torchlight fractured. The shadows dissolved and lost their shape. “What...?” he breathed. But his brother only smiled. He rose to his feet, even though his wounded body should have made that impossible, and looked directly into James’s eyes. “You have to go, James,” he said softly. He pointed toward a door that had appeared out of the darkness as if from nowhere. “This... wasn’t real,” James whispered. “It was,” his brother replied calmly. “These are your memories. But there is nothing to regret. Now go... and live.” James looked at the door. Then at his brother. The younger man smiled one last time—peaceful, almost at ease. James took a step forward and walked through the door. *The Passage of the Key — Aaliyah's Point of View* Aaliyah lay upon silk sheets, silently staring up at the ceiling of the tent. The day was only beginning. The sun still hung low above the horizon, and only faint light filtered through the tent walls, barely pushing back the remnants of the night’s darkness. The air was fragrant with incense. She was alone. As always. William had left before she had even opened her eyes. The only trace of his presence was the rumpled silk bedding beside her. Aaliyah closed her eyes and remembered the first time they had met. It had been in Vienta during the festivities, when the streets blazed with color, the air was rich with the scent of spices, and the rhythms of drums echoed from every corner of the city. Back then, Aaliyah had celebrated alongside the rest of the city, unaware that her life was about to change forever. William had come to the city under orders from his superiors. His mission was to seek out the local fire mages and secure their assistance for an upcoming expedition into the desert. Aaliyah first noticed him among a group of knights standing at the edge of the square. While the others looked around the bustling streets, trying to orient themselves in the unfamiliar city, William stood calm and self-assured, as though the surrounding chaos held no interest for him whatsoever. At the time, she did not even know his name. Yet she found herself unable to look away. There was something unusual about him— a quiet authority that naturally drew attention. Eventually, he noticed that she was watching him.Their eyes met. Instead of looking away, he smiled and walked straight toward her. Aaliyah was surprised by how naturally he moved through the crowd. People stepped aside without even realizing they were doing it. He stopped a few paces away. “Excuse me,” he said politely. “We’re looking for the local fire mages. Would you happen to know where we can find them?” Aaliyah smiled. “That depends on which one you're looking for.” William tilted his head slightly. “The best one.” Amusement sparkled in her eyes. “Then you're in luck.” She paused for a moment. “You've just found her.” One of the knights behind William let out a snort, but William’s expression barely changed. Only a flicker of interest appeared in his eyes. “You’re a fire mage?” “Does that surprise you?” “No.” His gaze briefly traveled over her. “I was simply expecting someone less attractive.” Aaliyah smiled in amusement. “And I was expecting someone more polite.” William’s smile widened. It was confident, carefree, and almost dangerously charming. It was obvious that her remark had not unsettled him in the slightest. “Then I apologize,” he said. Yet there was so much amusement in his voice that it was clear he did not regret his words at all. “And I hope I’ll be given the opportunity to make up for my behavior.” Aaliyah tilted her head slightly. “That will depend on how hard you're willing to try.” “Then I’ll do everything I can to convince you.” For a brief moment, William’s gaze drifted toward her lips. It lasted only an instant—so subtle that most people would have missed it. Aaliyah did not. “My name is William,” he said. “Aaliyah.” “A pleasure to meet you, Aaliyah.” He spoke her name slowly, with a faint smile, as though savoring the sound of it. Only later did she realize how easily he had won her over. Not with a single gesture, nor with a few carefully chosen words. It happened gradually. With every meeting. Every conversation. Every glance that lingered just a little longer than necessary. Yet there had always been a boundary between them—one William refused to cross. Once, she had leaned toward him and tried to kiss him. For a moment, it felt as though time itself had slowed. Then he pulled away. “I don't like that,” he said. Those words hurt her more than she had wanted to admit at the time. They were cold, brief, and final. Even so, she did not give up hope. A part of her heart continued to believe that one day something would change. That one day he would stop maintaining the distance he had so carefully built between them. It never happened. William never relented. He never gave her what she had hoped for. And then, one day, he simply left. Without an explanation. Without a goodbye. The feeling of abandonment wrapped around her like an iron grip. She sat down, breathing heavily, but no tears came. There was only silence. A crushing emptiness that surrounded her like a cold fog. “I loved him,” she whispered into the void. No one answered. Aaliyah lowered her gaze to the rumpled silk sheets. “I wanted to mean more to him.” Her voice trembled slightly. “More than just another woman he spent a few nights with and then forgot.” She fell silent. Then, unexpectedly, another face surfaced in her thoughts. James. He had never asked anything of her, and yet he had offered her so much. Only now did she realize how great the difference was between the man she had spent so long chasing and the one who had stood beside her all along. Aaliyah remained seated on the bed and slowly exhaled. The burden she had carried for so long seemed a little lighter. She realized how many emotions she had wasted on William. And now? Now it was time to leave the past behind and find her true happiness. Slowly, she rose to her feet, dressed herself, and stepped through the entrance of the tent with renewed determination. The moment she crossed the threshold, the world around her dissolved. The tent vanished. The morning light disappeared. The scent of incense faded into nothingness. Aaliyah found herself once more in the ancient, crumbling temple where time stood still. James and Rowena were already waiting for her.
