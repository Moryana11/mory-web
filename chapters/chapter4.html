Chapter IV. – The girl who burned

*Flashback from James’s point of view* James stood leaning against a tent pole, slowly cleaning the blade of his dagger. The cloth moved along the edge with slow, careful strokes. At the same time, he discreetly watched the woman sitting by the fire as she laughed at one of William’s remarks. Aaliyah. She was sincere, energetic, and kind in a way James rarely encountered. She could lift the spirits of the entire unit even when she herself was having a bad day. And beside her sat William. His fellow warrior was confident, eloquent, and popular. Whenever he entered a room, he attracted attention like a magnet, and Aaliyah was no exception. In battle, he was reliable. In camp, loud. In love... selfish. William liked attention. Admiration. The feeling that someone needed him. And Aaliyah gave him all of that without hesitation. James watched from a distance, as he always did. He never interfered. At first, she seemed happier, more alive. But as time passed, James began to notice how quickly her smile vanished after some of William’s comments. How hard she tried to become exactly what he wanted her to be. One morning, he saw her standing in front of an empty tent. William was gone. No goodbye. No apology. Only silence and cold emptiness. Aaliyah did not move. She simply stared ahead with the expression of someone who was still waiting for a person to return. James knew she did not need advice or questions right now. She only needed someone she could lean on. So that night, he approached her by the fire. “You deserve more than being someone’s trophy,” he told her quietly. She lowered her head. The silence between them was fragile. “What do you want, James?” she asked sharply. “Reassurance that you’re better than him?” He shook his head. “I don’t want anything from you. I just didn’t want to keep pretending I didn’t care.” The words left his mouth before he could stop them. He had never expected to say them out loud. Her eyes filled with something he recognized immediately—fear, pain, and a quiet *no*. “Don’t say that, James... Please,” she whispered. He respected her decision. He did not try to persuade her. Instead, he looked into her eyes one last time. “I’m not pressuring you,” he added quietly. “You don’t have to say anything. I just... wanted you to know.” After the mission, at the gates of Vienta, he looked into her eyes one final time and left. Two years later, he was tracking a monster through the desert—a beast that had massacred a merchant caravan. The tracks in the sand led him deep into the wasteland, where he spotted flashes of fire in the distance. He stopped at the top of a dune and immediately noticed two women fighting a sand lizard. And then he recognized her. Her fire could not be mistaken for anyone else’s. Not even after all those years. Without thinking, he quickened his pace and began running toward them. But before he reached them, Aaliyah unleashed one final stream of fire, and the lizard collapsed into the sand. James slowed down. He did not want to surprise them with a sudden approach while they were still shaken from the battle. So he eased his pace and continued more calmly, giving them time to notice him. Both women were already sitting beside a small fire when Aaliyah suddenly lifted her head and tightened her grip on her magical staff. Then their eyes met.
