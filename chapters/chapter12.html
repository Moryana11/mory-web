Chapter XII. – Shadow over the sands

The sun was slowly sinking toward the horizon, bathing the desert in a red-orange glow. Every step through the sand felt heavier, every breath of the dry air sharper. Yet when the outline of the oasis finally appeared in the distance, their hearts filled with relief. The Sanctuary Oasis emerged like a miracle amid the wasteland—nestled between steep cliffs whose ridges rose toward the sky like stone sentinels. From a bird's-eye view, it would have resembled an emerald gemstone set within the golden crown of the desert. The smooth cliff walls shielded the oasis from fierce winds and the eyes of unwanted visitors. It was this natural protection that had made it a safe haven for travelers from distant lands. Between groves of palm trees, a crystal-clear lake, and countless tents stretched a bustling marketplace—busy and vibrant like a beating heart. Caravans laden with goods arrived regularly from the surrounding trade routes. Heavy sacks of spices, fabrics, and precious metals were unloaded from camels while merchants arranged their wares in stalls filled with treasures from every corner of the world. The scent of dried dates, roasted nuts, and spiced tea mingled with the smell of sweat and leather. Every corner of the oasis was alive—merchants haggled over prices, children ran laughing between the stalls, and old storytellers shared tales that echoed all the way to the stone sanctuary at the far end of the settlement. The Sanctuary Oasis was more than a place of rest. It was a crossroads of worlds, a hidden nexus of ancient trade routes, and living proof that even in the middle of nowhere, something sacred could flourish. "That's it... the Sanctuary Oasis," Aaliyah whispered, a mixture of excitement and exhaustion in her voice. James nodded silently, his eyes never ceasing to scan the horizon. Though the beauty of the place was undeniable, his instincts warned him that something was wrong. Aaliyah stopped atop a dune. Turning into the wind, she closed her eyes. "The air... it's different. It smells of magic, but not only the pure kind." Rowena stepped beside her. "I feel it too. It's like residual energy—twisted and rotten." "It's the Shadow," James said quietly. "It's been following us ever since we left the Fragment of Time." Suddenly, the wind rose. Sand swirled upward, forming a towering column of dark dust and shadows. Within its center, the vague outline of a face began to emerge—nothing more than a shadow, a fleeting glimpse—and then two golden eyes ignited within it, piercing and cold. "Guardians of the orb," hissed a voice that sounded as though it were made of a thousand whispering voices speaking at once. "Exactly as foretold. Four seals... four keys... and you—Children of Light—will bring them directly to the threshold of the Fall." Aaliyah took a step back. "What are you?" "I am a messenger. Born from the remnants of Morr'kalil's will, hidden within the wind, the sand, and the silence between words." James stepped in front of the women and gripped the hilt of his dagger. "Then speak," he said through clenched teeth. "What exactly did you come here to tell us?" "A message," the figure whispered. Its voice deepened, becoming darker. "That you will die soon. Not quickly. Not mercifully..." James planted his feet firmly in the sand, his eyes dark and unwavering. "We'll see about that," he said quietly. For a brief moment, the Shadow fell silent. Then a cruel laugh echoed from its shapeless form. It sounded hollow, cracking like metal under immense pressure. "Yes... we shall see," it hissed as it slowly dissolved, fading away with a rustling sound like grinding stone. "The gates are opening... and the Seal is weakening. The time has come for the world to taste death once more." Silence followed. "That wasn't a warning," Rowena said, her voice trembling with fear. "It was a promise." She tightened her grip on the orb. Its gentle light pulsed like a beating heart. "We have to reach the Sanctuary Oasis. Maybe we'll find answers there—where to search for the remaining orbs... or someone who can tell us what we're supposed to do with them." "Or at least buy ourselves some time," James added. They wasted no time. The distance between them and the oasis shrank quickly as they crossed the dunes. With the coming of night, the sky turned violet, and the stars ignited overhead like witnesses to their fate. With every step, the Sanctuary Oasis drew nearer—and with it, the next chapter of their journey.
