Chapter III. – Fire and shadow

*Flashback from Aaliyah’s point of view* The golden light of the setting sun fell over the camp, where the euphoria of yet another victorious expedition was slowly fading into dust and weary gazes. Aaliyah sat on a rock with her legs crossed, her head resting in her hands. Her dress was singed from magic, her arms were tired, and her heart was beating faster than it should have. Behind her, she heard a voice she knew far too well. “You overdid it again, Red. That’s what happens when you try to save the whole world by yourself.” Aaliyah smiled despite herself—William. Eternally sarcastic, confident, and almost dangerously charming. Whenever he entered a room, he immediately drew everyone’s attention. It wasn’t just because of his position, but because of his natural charisma. He seemed attentive, perceptive, and at times unexpectedly kind. It only took him a few sentences to form an accurate picture of someone. He noticed the things others missed—small gestures, tones of voice, and unspoken worries. And because of that, he always knew which words would earn someone’s trust and which would make them do exactly what he needed. He approached her still wearing his armor, a shield on his back and a sword at his hip. His short brown hair was slightly tousled, and his neatly trimmed brown beard framed a distinctive, lightly tanned face with a smile that could seem either friendly or mocking. Aaliyah looked away and grumbled sarcastically. “Well, if I hadn’t used my magic at full strength, we’d probably all be dead right now… so thanks for your concern, hero.” William laughed. “I’m glad you’re so selfless... it must have exhausted you, huh?” he said, tilting his head provocatively. Then he sat down beside her, close enough that she could feel his warmth. Leaning toward her ear, he whispered, “I hope you’ve still got a little energy left... for tonight.” Aaliyah raised an eyebrow and frowned, but a faint smile soon spread across her face. That was how things had been between them for months—teasing, light flirting, passionate nights, and tension that never truly faded. And then one day she woke up, and he was gone. No goodbye. No explanation. The only thing left on the pillow was a brief note: I’m done with you. Aaliyah picked it up, her hands trembling. Her heart pounded so hard she felt as though she could hear every beat. Instinctively, she leapt from bed and ran to his tent, hoping he would be there—that maybe it was all just some stupid joke. But the tent was empty. All of his belongings were gone. Aaliyah stood in front of the vacant tent for a long time. In the distance, James watched her. He leaned against a small rock, hidden in the shadows. The warrior said nothing, but his eyes spoke volumes. Aaliyah noticed him and knew he had seen everything—how she laughed, how she loved, and how she fell. That evening, while she sat alone by the fire, James quietly approached. He sat beside her and, after a long silence, said: “You deserve more than being someone’s trophy.” Aaliyah studied him. There was something different in his gaze. It wasn’t just sympathy. For a moment, she thought she saw admiration there. Maybe respect. Maybe understanding. “What do you want, James?” she asked. “Reassurance that you’re better than him?” Anger slipped into her voice before she could stop it. She knew she was lashing out at the wrong person. It wasn’t his fault. It never had been. But James didn’t even move. He simply sat there, calm and sincere. And that irritated her more than anything. He shook his head. “I don’t want anything from you. I just didn’t want to keep pretending I didn’t care.” His words caught her off guard. For a moment, she could only stare at him in silence. Only then did she realize what he was truly offering her. Not flirtation. Not a game. Real feelings. “Don’t say that, James... Please,” she whispered. James fell silent for a moment before lowering his gaze. “I’m not pressuring you,” he added quietly. “You don’t have to say anything. I just... wanted you to know.” Then he straightened and stepped back. Just one step. As though that single movement closed everything that had been spoken between them. He turned and disappeared into the night, leaving her by the fire—in its warmth, in the crackling flames, alone with all the thoughts burning inside her chest. Two days later, they stood by the gates of Vienta, where the unit finally parted ways. Aaliyah could barely bring herself to look at him. “I’m sorry, James...” she said softly. James simply nodded. No anger. No disappointment. As though he had expected her answer from the very beginning. Silence settled between them for a moment. “I’ll come back,” he finally said calmly. “If you want me to.” And then he left. For two years, Aaliyah gathered the broken pieces of her heart and tried to put them back together. During the day, she ate, fought, and smiled—a perfect illusion she showed to the world. But at night, when everything grew quiet and she was left alone with her thoughts, painful memories of two men always returned. The first man was like a magnet, drawing everyone’s attenion wherever he went. Dominant, confident, and unwavering in his opinions. His words could be as charming as they were cruel—sharp, ruthless, and often aimed precisely where they would hurt the most. At times, Aaliyah felt special when she was with him. At others, she felt like a toy he could set aside the moment he lost interest. The second man was his complete opposite. Quiet, reserved, and difficult to read. Sometimes he would go for hours without saying a single word, simply watching her calmly. But James noticed the things everyone else overlooked. He handed her water before she even had the chance to ask for it. He draped a blanket over her shoulders on cold nights, even though he himself must have been freezing. And when she lacked the strength to speak, he never tried to force words out of her. For a long time, Aaliyah convinced herself that none of it mattered. That William had simply been a mistake. And that James was just another man whom time would eventually carry from her memory like all the others. Yet even after two years, she could not bring herself to hate one of them. Nor could she forget the other.
