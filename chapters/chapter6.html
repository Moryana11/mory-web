Chapter VI. – Cracks in the armor

The desert was silent once more. But it was not a peaceful silence—it was hollow, heavy, stretched taut like a string on the verge of snapping. Aaliyah stood there, breathing hard. Her shoulders trembled with every breath. The air around her still carried traces of fading magic—fiery, hissing, corrosive. Her last spell had been deadly. It had left behind nothing but silence and scorched earth. James stood a short distance away, silent, his spear still in hand. The blade was covered with remnants of black sand, yet he did not move. His gaze remained fixed on the spot where the creature had stood only moments before. As though he expected it to rise again. As though the fight was not truly over. His expression remained unreadable. Rowena stood beside Aaliyah, her hands trembling—not from exhaustion, but from what she had seen in the creature’s eyes just before it fell apart. There had not been emptiness in them. There had been pain. Rowena closed her eyes. “She... was suffering,” she whispered, barely audible. Aaliyah took a slow breath. The fire around her quieted, drawing closer to her body as though listening. “That doesn’t change anything,” she replied after a moment, her voice hard. “It would have killed us.” “I know,” Rowena nodded softly. But her gaze drifted once more to the creature’s crumbling remains. “Still... I feel a little sorry for it.” Aaliyah turned toward James. “The thing you were tracking,” she said, her voice sharpening, “before you found us... was it this?” “Yes. It attacked a caravan not far from here,” James answered calmly. “They hired me,” he continued. “The caravan was several days overdue, and no message had arrived. They were afraid something had happened, so I went looking for them.” For a moment, he lowered his eyes, as though piecing the scene back together in his mind. “First, I found wagon tracks. Then scattered goods. The canvas covers had been torn apart, the wooden frames splintered... and there was blood everywhere.” He paused. “At first, I thought a sand lizard had done it. The attack matched the pattern.” He took a short breath. “But then I found the bodies.” His expression darkened slightly. “At first glance, the bite marks looked the same. But something about them was wrong.” He fell silent for a moment. “That creature had far too many teeth in its mouth. The marks were crowded together, crooked, and irregular.” His eyes shifted toward the pile of black sand. “As if they were never supposed to be there at all.” Rowena blinked in confusion and looked at the creature’s remains. “That has nothing to do with a sand lizard,” she breathed in shock. “Sand lizards are normal living creatures. This... this was something completely different.” James reached into a pocket on his trousers. He hesitated for a moment, then pulled out a tooth. It was long and curved, and at first glance it appeared to belong to a sand lizard. Only upon closer inspection did it become obvious that something was wrong with it. The surface was twisted, deformed, and veined with fine black cracks. “I pulled this out of one of the bodies,” he said calmly. “That’s why I think it originally was a sand lizard.” Rowena frowned. “Couldn’t that tooth have belonged to another sand lizard?” she asked cautiously. “Maybe one that came to feed on the dead?” James slowly shook his head. Without a word, he handed the tooth to Aaliyah. She took it and examined it briefly. “It really does look similar to a sand lizard’s tooth,” she said calmly. “But only similar.” Her gaze traveled over the warped surface. “This tooth developed incorrectly. It’s twisted. Normal sand lizards don’t have teeth like this.” Without further hesitation, she handed it to Rowena. The moment it touched her palm, she flinched slightly. The light surrounding her trembled and thickened, as if it had encountered something unwilling to yield. She closed her eyes and focused entirely on the sensation for a moment. “I can feel dark magic coming from it,” she said quietly. “It’s old. Very old.” She opened her eyes, concern visible in her expression. “I’ve never felt anything like it before.” James nodded. “That’s the same impression I got at the caravan,” he replied softly. Aaliyah crossed her arms and stared into the darkness of the desert for a moment. “It’s not wise to travel at night. We’ll wait until morning,” she said firmly. Then her gaze hardened. “But let’s make one thing clear.” The flames in the campfire crackled softly, as if responding to the change in her tone. “This isn’t just some stray beast. If old dark magic is involved, if someone awakened or created something like this...” She paused briefly. “We should report it.” “In Vienta?” Rowena asked. “Yes. Or at the nearest oasis,” Aaliyah nodded. “Caravans pass through here all the time. And this thing wasn’t killing out of hunger.” James remained silent for a moment before slowly nodding. “I agree. First, we find out where the trail leads. And if there’s more than one creature...” “...then it should be reported to Stonegard Castle,” Rowena finished quietly. Aaliyah looked toward the remains of the dead creature. “Should we try to take it with us?” James answered immediately. “I’d rather not.” His gaze shifted to the scattered bones and black sand. “I’m not eager to drag something soaked in ancient dark magic across the desert.” After a moment, Rowena pulled a small glass vial with a silver cap from her satchel. “I can at least take a small sample,” she offered quietly. “My magic can keep it isolated.” James gave a brief nod. “That should be enough.” Rowena carefully knelt beside the remains and used her magic to lift a few grains of black sand and a tiny bone fragment into the vial. The moment she sealed it, the light around the glass flickered faintly. Meanwhile, Aaliyah watched the remains with a cold expression. “I’ll burn the rest, just to be safe.” She extended her hand, and flames immediately ignited around her fingers. The fire illuminated the sand surrounding the creature’s remains—and also the body of the sand lizard lying nearby. “Wait,” James said calmly. Aaliyah turned toward him. James nodded toward the lizard. “Leave that one.” Aaliyah raised an eyebrow slightly. “Why?” “Because we can eat it.” “Eat it?” Rowena repeated in disbelief. “You want to eat a sand lizard?” “Yes,” James replied with complete seriousness. “They usually taste pretty good. Although...” His gaze briefly slid toward the charred lizard. “I’m not sure this one will still taste good after Aaliyah’s modifications.” Aaliyah narrowed her eyes. “What exactly is that supposed to mean?” “It means you’re a terrible cook,” he answered without hesitation. “And since you’ve already roasted it so thoroughly, it would be a shame to waste it.” “I didn’t roast it. I was trying to kill it.” “The result is surprisingly similar,” he remarked dryly. “Once during a mission, she nearly poisoned the entire unit.” “That wasn’t my fault,” Aaliyah shot back immediately. “Three people had stomach cramps for two days,” James continued calmly. “That happened once.” “Once was enough.” Rowena tried to hide an amused smile. Aaliyah shot James an offended look and crossed her arms. “You know you’re insufferable?” “You say that every time I’m right.” “No. I say it all the time.” For a brief moment, amusement flickered at the corner of James’s mouth before disappearing again. Aaliyah snorted. “I’m starting to understand why you travel alone.” “And I’m starting to understand why they banned you from cooking back then,” James replied completely calmly. This time, Rowena couldn’t hold back her laughter.
