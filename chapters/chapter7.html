Chapter VII. – A Fragment of the time

As the sun climbed high above the horizon, its rays no longer warmed—they scorched. The air shimmered above the sand, and each step felt more exhausting than the last. The landscape around them remained silent and empty. Then something changed. At first, it was only a strange sensation. A faint tremor of magic at the very edge of perception. So faint that none of them were certain whether they were truly sensing it or merely imagining it. “Do you feel that?” Aaliyah asked quietly, as if she were afraid that speaking too loudly might shatter something fragile. She stood a step ahead of the others, her eyes narrowed against the sun, a thin layer of sweat on her brow—not from the heat. She felt her stomach tighten and her heart begin to beat uneasily. Something moved at the edge of her vision. She snapped her head toward it. The desert was still there. And yet it wasn't. For a brief moment, she saw dozens of people among the dunes. Men, women, and children. They were kneeling in the sand, their faces twisted with fear. A second later, they vanished as though they had never existed. Aaliyah froze. Another flash. Knights in armor stood atop a dune. Behind them, fires raged, and thick smoke rose into the sky. Yet the moment she focused on them, they dissolved into the shimmering haze. A distant scream echoed through the air. Or at least she thought it did. Ahead of them, a stone altar stained with blood appeared for an instant. Figures in dark robes stood around it, chanting words she could not understand in a monotonous rhythm. Bodies lay at their feet. Then the vision disappeared once more. Only the endless desert and the blazing sun remained. Aaliyah slowly turned toward Rowena. It seemed she had seen the same thing. “This isn’t an ordinary magical illusion,” Rowena said gravely. “It’s a Time Fragment.” “A Time Fragment?” Aaliyah asked, confused. “Yes,” Rowena continued. “They are memories of events that certain places carry within them like scars. Sometimes those memories awaken—they become tangible, almost alive.” “But who could have—” Aaliyah began. Suddenly, the ground split apart with a terrifying crack—and darkness swallowed them. In the depths of the black void, everything began to change. The shadows thickened, rippled, and slowly took shape, forming images of long-forgotten events. A few moments later, they were no longer standing in the present. They had found themselves in the midst of one of the darkest chapters in history—a war against a fanatical cult nearly five centuries in the past. It was an age when the land groaned beneath the suffering of its people. Every drop of blood, every cry of pain seeped into its depths like poison. The earth twisted in agony, cracking and trembling as though it felt every malicious act committed upon its surface. Nature itself begged for mercy. The world had nearly fallen to its knees before a god whose name no one dared to speak. Morr'kalil. The dark god of blood, suffering, and death. He knew neither compassion nor mercy. He found profound satisfaction in the suffering of mortals. He longed for a world that would be nothing more than an endless wound that could never heal. His followers believed that the world was irredeemably corrupted and that only through suffering could it be purified. Morr'kalil was their truth. Their law. Their savior within the flames. He dwelled in the Darkness—a void beyond the boundary of death, where lost souls drowned in the mists of oblivion. He fed upon them. Their pain sustained him. But he wanted more. He wanted the living. He wanted to turn the entire world into a burning temple of agony. His followers swept across the world like a plague. They searched for four sacred sites where the boundary between this world and Morr'kalil's realm was at its thinnest, so they could perform sacrifices there. It did not matter who was sacrificed. Morr'kalil did not discriminate. Anyone would do. Anyone with a tongue capable of crying for help and a body capable of bleeding. The victims were dragged to the altars. Chains bound their wrists. Their heads were forced toward the ground. They screamed, begged for mercy, gasped for breath as terror twisted their faces. And around them... the crowd danced. The fanatics stood in circles around the sacrifices, their faces warped by depraved madness and religious zeal that consumed every part of their being. Around their necks hung heavy amulets—cold, darkly gleaming metal discs engraved with a lensless eye that seemed to watch every movement with merciless emptiness. Their chanting was fervent, almost ecstatic, transformed into prayers of pain and destruction born from their obsessive belief that every cut and every drop of blood was a sacred rite cleansing the world. They laughed cruelly, with a fury that tore at the soul and twisted the heart within the darkness of their fanatic passion. The bodies of the sacrifices were mutilated with such precision that it seemed almost...beautiful. But it was not beauty meant for the world of men. It was beauty meant for Morr'kalil. The shadows moved. The ground was red. The air burned. And the silence between the screams carried the weight of stone. Nothing ever grew in those places again. Magic fractured. Light avoided the land. The air tasted like ash. Time itself seemed to lose its meaning. And in the age of greatest darkness, when three rituals had already been completed and the fourth was meant to become the crown of Morr'kalil's return, a desperate act took place. The Resistance was born. The three nations of the continent of Solisium—Laslan, Stonegard, and Talandre—joined forces for the first time in history. They were united not by hope, but by desperation. Desperation born from the knowledge that if they failed, the world would be consumed by endless suffering. It was a war unlike any that had come before. It was not fought for land, a crown, or faith. It was fought for the very soul of the world—for its right to know sunlight without destruction, love without pain, and death without the mockery of the dark god Morr'kalil. Battles raged across endless plains, through twisting underground passages, upon storm-torn seas, and in the skies above. Ancient cities that had witnessed forgotten ages fell in flames. Tens of thousands of warriors—knights, mages, and mercenaries alike—met their deaths amid blood, shadows, smoke, and screams. And in the end came the decisive battle in the Marsh of Silence, a place where the currents of time, memory, and will converged. The boundary between worlds was thinner there than anywhere else. It was there that the fate of the world was decided. The army of the Resistance drove back Morr'kalil's final followers, who came like apparitions of death. There, they performed the Sealing Ritual—a complex and dangerous ceremony whose power was meant to cast the dark god back into the abyss from which he had emerged. The gateway between the world and the Darkness closed. Time within the Marsh of Silence fractured and turned upon itself. Those who survived began to see things that were never meant to be seen. Memories faded and rewrote themselves. Past and present intertwined in a terrifying dance. The boundary between reality and madness began to dissolve. But Morr'kalil...did not forget. The vision slowly faded and dispersed like mist before giving way to another. This time, Rowena, Aaliyah, and James found themselves standing within an ancient ruined temple. The pillars were broken, the walls covered in mold, and the shadows cast strange patterns. The air was heavy and stale. At the center of the silent ruin stood a mirror. It was tall and ancient, its frame decorated with engraved symbols that pulsed with a faint, dark light. The surface of the glass was not smooth. Instead, it seemed to ripple and shift as though the mirror itself were breathing. Those who gazed into it saw not only their reflection, but something more. “I recognize this mirror,” Rowena whispered. Her voice sounded different, as though something were muffling it. “This is the Mirror of Shadows. It shows not only what is and what was... but also what may be.” Aaliyah and James remained silent. Slowly, they stepped closer, their footsteps echoing through the empty hall like droplets of water falling onto stone. They stopped beside Rowena. And then they looked. Rowena saw herself in the mirror. And yet it was not her. The reflection was distorted and unfamiliar, as though it were a darker, lost part of her soul. Her eyes were devoid of light. Hair hung matted against her face. Dress was torn and hands were covered in blood. She sat alone in a ruined city surrounded by bodies—not enemies, but allies. Her parents lay motionless in her lap, burned and lifeless, their eyes empty. Rowena screamed, but no sound emerged. She clung to their bodies, trying to heal them, but her magic crumbled between her fingers like dust. “No... no...” she whispered aloud. Her hand shot forward — but the mirror remained cold and impenetrable. Aaliyah stood at the edge of a vast abyss. A cold wind lashed her face, and pain, fear, and helplessness filled her eyes. Her face was pale and rigid, as though she had long forgotten how to smile. Every line of her expression carried a silent plea for relief—a desire to leap into the darkness below, to end her suffering, to finally escape the burden that consumed her from within and broke her apart piece by piece. The mirror showed her the embodiment of her deepest fear — the fear of a love that brought only pain instead of joy. Aaliyah stumbled backward as though the mirror had burned her. Her eyes widened, and her lips trembled. “That’s not me...” she whispered. Yet deep inside, she knew it was. It was the part of herself she had spent so long trying to suppress. She swallowed hard and pressed a hand against her chest, as though trying to hold together a heart that beat too hard, too painfully. “I don’t want this anymore...” she breathed shakily. “I don’t want to fall in love again only to end up disappointed once more.” But the mirror remained silent. And her reflection continued to stand there — alone, broken, ready to jump. James stood before the mirror. His reflection revealed a dark future born from unreturned love and loss. Within the vision, he found himself in an empty hall where thick shadows smothered the light. At the center of the darkness knelt Aaliyah. Her body trembled. Her eyes were red from crying. Her face was pale as ash. Her hair hung across her face, and her shoulders shook beneath the weight of despair. Standing above her was William, wearing a malicious smile. He was enjoying her suffering. James closed his eyes. When he opened them again, another image appeared. The hall was empty. Aaliyah lay upon the floor. Dead. She had taken poison. Within the silence of the hall, her death screamed louder than any words ever could. He closed his eyes a second time. The mirror changed again. William lay dead upon the ground. His skin was pale, almost gray. His lips were blue. Blood mixed with foam slowly seeped from his mouth. James knew exactly how William had died. He recognized death caused by poison. A poison he himself had used to kill many times before. Fast. Silent. Invisible. James stood above William's body, his eyes empty and devoid of regret. He had done it because he could not bear losing Aaliyah. Because he could never have her. Because she had never chosen him. James stood before the mirror, his gaze steady and resolute. He was not frightened by what he had seen. He understood how deep his love for Aaliyah truly ran. And how far he was willing to go in order to protect her. The mirror darkened. The engravings faded. And the light died away. The hall sank into a heavy silence. But the vision was not over. They remained standing within the ancient, crumbling temple. Their breaths caught in their throats, as though the air around them had thickened into lead. And then it happened. It was as if the boundary between vision and reality had cracked. The images revealed by the mirror did not disappear. Instead, they seeped into them. Aaliyah and Rowena looked different. Not as they knew themselves. But as the mirror had shown them. Rowena could barely remain standing. The light had vanished from her eyes, leaving them empty, darkened, lifeless. As though what she had witnessed still echoed within them. Her hair hung heavy and matted against her face, soaked with sweat and blood. Fresh crimson drops trickled downward, mingling with the remnants of magic. Her dress, once dignified, was torn and stained with the dust of the place and the weight of horrors she could not name. Beside her, Aaliyah looked as though she belonged to another world. Her face had become so pale that she resembled a shadow of herself. Dark circles lay beneath her eyes. Her hair hung loose and disheveled, tangled with tears. Her wrinkled, damp dress hung loosely from her frame. Meanwhile, a faint shadow emerged from the cracked mirror. It was not tangible. More like a cold mist slowly spreading through the space around them. Suffocating. Persistent. A memory refusing to let go, even though it should have been forgotten long ago. Rowena trembled and wrapped her arms around herself as though fighting a pain that gripped her heart. “We’re trapped,” she whispered, her voice shaking. “This Time Fragment will keep us here until it breaks us apart.” Aaliyah pressed her palms against her temples, trying to suppress the rising panic. “No. There has to be something. Some way...” James stood silently, his cold eyes following the mist as it spread around them. “Panic won’t help us much right now,” he said in a voice that left no room for doubt. “If we found our way in here, then there has to be a way out as well. We just have to find it.” The shadow emerging from the cracked mirror began to thicken and slowly grow larger. “And we should move quickly,” James added. “We don’t have much time.”
