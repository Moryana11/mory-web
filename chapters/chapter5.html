Chapter V. – Fire, shadow and water

The desert wind changed. Rowena noticed it first—not physically, but through her magic. The unpleasant trembling left behind by the battle with the lizard, which she had felt in the sand around her until now, suddenly faded away. It was replaced by pressure. Invisible. Focused. A presence that belonged neither to the living nor the dead. Something in between. A figure slowly approached. A long spear in one hand, daggers at his belt, and light black armor. Short dark hair tousled by the wind. A face carved from stone with short stubble and black eyes that held death within them. Beside her, Aaliyah stopped moving. Her fingers tightened slightly around her staff, but not because she was preparing to defend herself. Because of him. “Who is that?” Rowena whispered. “James,” Aaliyah answered quietly. When he finally reached them, he stood silently for a moment. He knew very well what a single wrong word could cause. Aaliyah moved first. Slowly, tension in her shoulders, but no fear in her eyes. Only an old uncertainty. “You’ve been gone for a long time,” she said. “I was following tracks,” he replied quietly. “Mine?” “Yours. And the tracks of something that doesn’t belong in the desert.” Rowena could feel the tension between them. Part of her thought she should leave and give them privacy. But another part could not suppress her curiosity. She wanted to understand what kind of relationship existed between them. So she stayed where she was. James quietly sat down by the fire across from Aaliyah. His black armor rustled softly as he rested his hands on his knees and stared into the flames without a word. His face was half-hidden in shadow, but his eyes—dark and deep—followed the dance of the fire with a strange calmness. Aaliyah lowered her gaze and began playing with the strap of her boot. “What did you mean when you said you were tracking something that doesn’t belong in the desert?” Rowena finally asked, unable to restrain her curiosity any longer. “Darkness,” he replied. “Darkness that crawled out from the depths beneath the Moonlight Desert.” “So now you hunt dark specters instead of people?” Aaliyah smirked. “Specters don’t have souls. They don’t scream when they die,” he answered without humor. Aaliyah lowered her eyes. She had no response to that. Silence settled over the three of them. The fire crackled softly while the wind played with the sand atop the dunes. Then a strange sound echoed through the night. Deep. Distant. Unnatural. As if it were coming from somewhere beneath their feet. A night creature slowly emerged from the ground—a being formed from sand, bones, and magic older than the desert itself. Its eyes burned with blue light. It emitted a sound that tore at their ears. All three rose to their feet. Aaliyah raised her staff. Behind her, Rowena quietly whispered a protective spell. And James lifted his spear without saying a word. “Someone has got to be joking,” Aaliyah muttered aloud. “First the lizard... and now this.” “Focus,” James said calmly, tightening his grip on the spear. The creature immediately charged at them. With every step, parts of its sandy body crumbled apart and reassembled themselves, while black energy pulsed between the exposed bones, holding the entire being together. Aaliyah thrust out her staff, and a stream of fire erupted into the night. It slammed into the creature’s chest, scattering part of its body in every direction. Yet the creature kept coming as if it felt no pain. Rowena quickly retreated and began weaving protective magic around them. Pale blue runes lit up above the sand just as the monster swung a massive skeletal arm. Without a word, James rushed forward. With a single movement, he knocked the attack aside. The creature roared and lunged directly at him. James dodged just before its jaws snapped shut and drove his spear deep into its side. Shadow magic immediately spread from the spearhead through the cracks in the creature’s bones, disrupting the black energy that held its body together. Aaliyah seized the opportunity and unleashed another wave of fire. This time, the flames struck its head directly. The creature twisted violently, released one final inhuman cry, and collapsed to the ground. The black energy sustaining it dissolved into nothingness, leaving behind only bare bones resting in a heap of black sand.
